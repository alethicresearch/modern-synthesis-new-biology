Working model

Information value, not a one-for-one replacement test

Current regulator-authored cases support a narrower proposition: target and model relevance help determine whether the clinical molecule can generate informative direct animal evidence. When direct relevance fails, the regulatory response is not uniform. It can include surrogate-animal toxicology, selective purpose-built animal studies, human tissue or cell systems, genomic or computational evidence, prior knowledge, clinical mitigation, or omission of conventional studies.

The key open question is therefore not simply whether a therapy class “uses animals” or “uses NAMs,” but how the total evidence package changes when particular legacy studies lose decision-relevant information value.

Methods + provenance

How the living evidence companion is maintained

The paper is the stable scholarly argument; this site is the larger evidence record. Data layers are kept separate so a policy announcement, a qualified tool, a named product decision, and a measured reduction cannot silently become the same kind of evidence.

Evidence hierarchy

Primary agency, legislative, regulator-authored, and regulator-hosted sources are preferred. Sponsor claims that lack regulator confirmation remain in research-side candidate files and are not rendered here as verified adoption.

Stage discipline

Accepted into a pathway is not qualified. Draft guidance is not final guidance. Collaboration is not regulatory acceptance. Funding is not implementation. A NAM can support a decision without demonstrably displacing an animal study.

Prospective separation

Forward milestones live in a separate dataset and are visibly labeled as recommendations, forecasts, or catalytic proposals. They never become historical facts without a separately sourced observed event.

Publication freeze

At manuscript submission, the evidence state used for the paper should be tagged and archived as a dated release. The live resource can then continue to evolve without changing the evidentiary basis reviewed with the manuscript.
