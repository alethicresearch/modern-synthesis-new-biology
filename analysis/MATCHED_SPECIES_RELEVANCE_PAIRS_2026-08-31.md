# Matched Species-Relevance Pairs — 2026-08-31

## Why matched pairs

The broader comparator set suggests that pharmacological/model relevance is associated with whether conventional general/repeat-dose toxicology is retained, omitted or radically tailored. Because that set mixes modalities, a stronger descriptive test is to compare products that are closer in biological function and were reviewed by the same regulator.

These are **illustrative matched comparisons**, not randomized or statistically representative pairs. They reduce some obvious confounding but do not eliminate differences in molecular design, indication, development era or applicable guidance.

## Pair 1: complement C5 antibodies — same target, same regulator, different species relevance

### Soliris (eculizumab) / Ultomiris (ravulizumab): clinical antibody not pharmacologically active in animals

Health Canada's Soliris review states that eculizumab is specific for human C5 and that no other species exhibited a pharmacologically active response. Toxicity testing therefore used BB5.1, a surrogate anti-mouse C5 antibody, including 4-week and 26-week repeat-dose studies and reproductive/developmental studies.

Primary source:
`https://dhpp.hpfb-dgpsa.ca/review-documents/resource/SBD00136`

Health Canada's Ultomiris review likewise states that ravulizumab does not bind non-human C5 and that no animal species has a pharmacologically active response. A murine C5 surrogate was used for a 26-week repeat-dose study and reproductive studies.

Primary source:
`https://dhpp.hpfb-dgpsa.ca/review-documents/resource/SBD00463`

### Piasky (crovalimab): cynomolgus C5 is pharmacologically relevant

Health Canada reports concentration- and pH-dependent binding of crovalimab to both human and cynomolgus C5 and identifies cynomolgus monkey as the only pharmacologically relevant species. The accepted package retained 5-week and 6-month repeat-dose cynomolgus toxicology plus an enhanced prenatal/postnatal development study.

Primary source:
`https://dhpp.hpfb-dgpsa.ca/review-documents/resource/SBD1751980281334`

### Interpretation

This is a particularly clean same-target contrast:

- **eculizumab/ravulizumab:** no directly relevant animal species → surrogate-mouse toxicology;
- **crovalimab:** directly relevant cynomolgus species → direct NHP toxicology.

The comparison strongly supports the narrower proposition that **species relevance changes which animal model is treated as capable of supplying regulatory information**. It does not by itself predict whether regulators will choose surrogate toxicology versus human-relevant omission when no directly relevant species exists.

## Pair 2: CD3 bispecific T-cell engagers — same broad modality, same regulator, different species relevance

### Blincyto (blinatumomab): no ordinary relevant experimental species

Health Canada states that blinatumomab binds human and chimpanzee B/T cells but not lymphocytes of other animal species. Apart from limited short-duration chimpanzee work, most toxicology was conducted in mice using a surrogate molecule engineered to bind murine CD19 and CD3. The surrogate supported a comprehensive panel of general and specialized toxicology studies, with mouse studies extending to 13 weeks.

Primary source:
`https://dhpp.hpfb-dgpsa.ca/review-documents/resource/SBD00207`

### Imdelltra (tarlatamab): cynomolgus target binding retained

Health Canada states that monkey CD3 and DLL3 are sufficiently similar to the human targets for tarlatamab binding and selected cynomolgus monkeys for toxicological analysis. The accepted package included 10-day, one-month and three-month repeat-dose cynomolgus studies. A murine surrogate was also used for a one-month mouse study because tarlatamab recognizes murine DLL3 but not murine CD3.

Primary source:
`https://dhpp.hpfb-dgpsa.ca/review-documents/resource/SBD1739374529873/`

### Interpretation

This pair reinforces two points at once:

1. **Direct target cross-reactivity changes the available evidence architecture.** Blinatumomab required a surrogate construct for most general toxicology; tarlatamab could be dosed directly in cynomolgus monkeys.
2. **Evidence architectures can be hybrid.** Even tarlatamab used a murine surrogate for an additional question despite having a directly relevant NHP species. The correct unit of analysis is therefore the regulatory question, not simply the product.

## The strongest revised hypothesis

The matched examples suggest a hierarchy of questions:

**A. Is the clinical molecule pharmacologically relevant in an available animal species?**

- If yes, direct relevant-species toxicology is often retained.
- If no, a direct conventional program becomes scientifically problematic.

**B. If not, what does the regulator/sponsor do instead?**

Possible responses now documented in the corpus include:

- create a surrogate molecule and retain an animal toxicology architecture;
- retain only animal studies that answer narrower product-specific questions;
- rely on human tissue/cell, iPSC, organotypic, genomic or computational evidence;
- use prior knowledge and clinical mitigation; or
- omit the conventional study.

This second choice appears to depend on more than species relevance alone, including modality, risk question, regulator/jurisdiction and the maturity of alternative evidence.

## International-policy implication

The March 2026 MHRA policy makes the surrogate branch especially important. MHRA states that when a drug substance is not pharmacologically active in animals, it does **not** support animal testing including with surrogate molecules and can assess scientifically justified non-animal evidence instead.

Primary source:
`https://www.gov.uk/guidance/mhra-approach-to-medicines-using-non-animal-methods`

That differs from the evidence architecture accepted in Health Canada's Soliris, Ultomiris, Blincyto and even 2025 Tzield reviews. This should be framed as **regulatory divergence in the preferred response to species non-relevance**, not as proof that one regulator would reject the other's submitted package.

## What to test next

A systematic analysis should separate two outcomes:

1. **Direct species relevance outcome:** direct relevant-species toxicology versus no direct relevant species.
2. **Response-to-nonrelevance outcome:** surrogate animal toxicology versus selective animal retention versus human-relevant tailored evidence versus conventional-study omission.

The next sampling frame should intentionally include same-target or same-modality product families wherever possible. Particularly useful families are complement inhibitors, CD3 bispecifics, anti-CD3 antibodies, checkpoint antibodies and advanced cellular therapies.

If replicated, this design can support a stronger manuscript claim than a catalogue of NAM examples: **model relevance governs the information value of legacy studies, while regulatory policy governs what evidence architecture replaces them when that information value collapses.**
