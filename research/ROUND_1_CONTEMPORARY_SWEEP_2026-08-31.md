## Purpose

This pass extends the 2022–2026 evidence corpus beyond the manuscript-derived chronology. It prioritizes developments that clarify movement from institutional commitment through qualification infrastructure to adoption. Primary sources are preferred, and stage distinctions are preserved.

## High-confidence additions / corrections

### NIH human-based science initiative — 2025-04-29
Primary source: https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/nih-prioritize-human-based-research-technologies

NIH announced a new initiative to expand human-based science while reducing animal use. The announcement stated an intention to establish ORIVA; expand funding, training, and infrastructure for non-animal approaches; add evaluation criteria addressing suitability, context of use, translatability, and human relevance; train grant-review staff regarding potential bias toward animal studies; integrate alternative-method experts into study sections; and publicly report research spending annually.

Classification: observed; commitment / review-infrastructure.
Materiality: high; major milestone candidate.
Why it matters: this is the institutional commitment event that precedes later implementation. The existing ledger's approximate July 2025 NIH-priority event should be corrected to April 29, 2025 rather than treated as a July announcement.

### NIH funding-opportunity policy implementation — 2025-07-10
Primary source: https://grants.nih.gov/news-events/nih-extramural-nexus-news/2025/07/nih-funding-announcements-to-align-with-nih-initiative-to-prioritize-human-based-research

NIH stated that new NOFOs relating to animal model systems must also support human-focused approaches, and that NIH would no longer issue NOFOs exclusively supporting animal models or prescribe the model type that must be used. Institutes and Centers may issue NOFOs excluding animal use entirely.

Classification: observed; adoption / funding-policy.
Materiality: high; major milestone candidate.
Why it matters: this is implementation of the April commitment in the machinery of grant funding, not merely another statement of intent. It should be represented separately from the April 29 initiative.

### NIH clarification of model-choice policy — 2025-07-18
Primary source: https://grants.nih.gov/news-events/nih-extramural-nexus-news/2025/07/how-does-the-nih-initiative-to-prioritize-human-based-research-affect-research-proposing-the-use-of-laboratory-animals

NIH clarified that researchers remain free to propose animal, human, in vitro, computational, real-world-data, or combined approaches; peer review should assess scientific merit, relevance to human disease, and suitability for the research question. Animal use remains permitted when scientifically appropriate and justified.

Classification: supporting implementation / clarification rather than a separate major milestone.
Materiality: medium.
Why it matters: prevents overstatement of the July 10 policy as a prohibition on animal-model research.

### NIH Standardized Organoid Modeling Center — 2025-09-25
Primary source: https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/nih-establishes-nations-first-dedicated-organoid-development-center-reduce-reliance-animal-modeling

NIH announced contracts totaling $87 million for the first three years to establish the Standardized Organoid Modeling Center at Frederick National Laboratory. Its explicit objectives include standardized and reproducible organoid protocols, open access to protocols/data/organoids, large-scale production, and collaboration with FDA toward models meeting preclinical testing standards. Initial organ targets are liver, lung, heart, and intestine.

Classification: observed; qualification / validation-infrastructure.
Materiality: high; major milestone candidate.
Why it matters: directly addresses reproducibility, standardization, accessibility, and regulator-readiness — precisely the infrastructure the paper identifies as necessary between invention and adoption.

### FDA first in-silico ISTAND LOI — 2026-06-03
Primary source: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fda-accepts-first-silico-drug-development-tool-under-istand-program-help-predict-drug-induced-liver

FDA CDER accepted the first LOI for an in-silico DDT into ISTAND: an AI-driven digital liver model proposed for prediction of DILI. The tool compares chemical structures of candidates against historical reference drugs with known DILI risk.

Classification: observed; qualification pipeline.
Materiality: high; major milestone candidate.
Critical distinction: accepted LOI, not qualified DDT.
Why it matters: together with the 2024 Liver-Chip LOI, it shows multiple human-relevant technology classes entering the same reusable qualification architecture.

### EPA accepted-NAM expansion and nomination mechanism — 2026-06-02
Primary source: https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/trump-epa-takes-new-action-eliminate-animal-testing

EPA announced the first update in five years to its alternative-method list for TSCA chemical assessments and introduced a streamlined nomination process for additional NAMs. This action is explicitly connected to the agency's 2035 mammalian-testing goal.

Classification: observed; qualification / adoption-enabling regulatory infrastructure.
Materiality: high; major milestone.
Why it matters: creates both an expanded usable-method set and a continuing intake mechanism. Retain the distinction between inclusion on an accepted-method list and documented use in a specific decision; the latter is represented separately by the Aug. 27, 2026 implementation event.

### European Commission chemical-safety roadmap — 2026-06-01
Primary sources:
- https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/publications/roadmap-towards-phasing-out-animal-testing-chemical-safety-assessments_en
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=intcom:C(2026)3497

The Commission adopted C(2026)3497, a roadmap toward gradually replacing animal testing for chemical safety assessments. It spans 15 legislative domains and includes more than 30 recommendations. The Commission's implementation page also establishes a Roadmap Steering Team mechanism.

Classification: observed; commitment with explicit implementation architecture.
Materiality: high; major milestone.
Why it matters: unusually broad cross-legislative commitment and a useful international comparator to US sector-by-sector change.

### UK Replacing Animals in Science strategy — 2025-11-11
Primary sources:
- https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/replacing-animals-in-science-strategy
- https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/replacing-animals-in-science-strategy/replacing-animals-in-science-a-strategy-to-support-the-development-validation-and-uptake-of-alternative-methods

The UK government published a five-year strategy covering development, validation, regulatory confidence, adoption, infrastructure, investment, and targeted replacement. Its long-term vision is elimination of animal use except in exceptional circumstances, while explicitly acknowledging current areas where reliable alternatives are not yet available.

Classification: observed; commitment.
Materiality: high; major milestone.
Why it matters: the strategy is particularly useful for the paper because it explicitly treats development, validation, and uptake as separate problems and includes time-bounded implementation commitments.

### Complement-ARIE VQN role clarification
Primary source: https://www.nih.gov/common-fund/common-fund-programs/complement-animal-research-experimentation-complement-arie-program/complement-arie-frequently-asked-questions

NIH states that the VQN is intended to accelerate deployment and regulatory approval by establishing and applying validation/qualification frameworks, standardized reporting/common data elements, conformity assessments, and quality-management systems. NIH explicitly states that the VQN itself will **not** validate or qualify specific NAMs; qualification/acceptance remains with the relevant regulatory authorities and can differ by agency, center, context of use, jurisdiction, or country.

Classification: clarification to existing VQN event.
Why it matters: reinforces the manuscript's distinction between infrastructure for qualification and the legal/regulatory act of qualification or acceptance itself.

That claim was **not verified by the sources found in this sweep** and should not be silently reconciled with the February resolution. Next action: locate the July 27 primary board record or revise/remove that ledger event if the July claim cannot be substantiated.

## Analytical implications emerging from this sweep

The observed NIH sequence is more structured than the present manuscript chronology suggests:

1. institutional commitment (Apr. 29, 2025),
2. funding-rule implementation (Jul. 10, 2025),
3. dedicated reproducibility/standardization infrastructure (Sep. 25, 2025),
4. large-scale development and validation awards (Mar. 18, 2026),
5. centralized institutional coordination through ORIVA (Jun. 15, 2026).

This is close to a natural experiment in how commitment becomes infrastructure and then potentially adoption. The next manuscript revision should consider using this sequence as a concrete worked example rather than distributing the events across unrelated sections.

FDA likewise now shows parallel entries into a reusable qualification pathway: the 2024 Liver-Chip LOI and the 2026 AI digital-liver LOI. Their value is not that either tool is qualified — neither is on the evidence reviewed here — but that distinct technology classes are being routed through the same formal qualification architecture.
