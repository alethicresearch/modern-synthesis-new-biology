# Research Round 1 — International product precedents and PMDA streamlining

Date: 2026-08-31

## Objective

Test whether the emerging U.S. adoption findings are jurisdiction-specific or whether the same logic appears in public assessment records and regulator positions outside the United States. Preserve a strict distinction between named product decisions, nonbinding regulator thinking, and final guidance.

## Kimmtrak as a cross-jurisdiction test case

Kimmtrak (tebentafusp) is now supported by public records from FDA, EMA and TGA.

### FDA

FDA/CDER/OND reviewers identify Kimmtrak as a NAM regulatory-decision example. Human tissue and cell-based assays assessed target/species specificity, human-tissue cross-reactivity and organ-specific toxicity potential. FDA authors state that these data supported clinical-trial safety in the absence of pharmacologically relevant animal models.

FDA review: https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2022/761228Orig1s000MultidisciplineR.pdf
FDA-authored synthesis: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10915818251384270

### European Medicines Agency

EMA product information states that tebentafusp is human-specific and that there are no relevant animal species in which nonclinical toxicology could be tested. It also records that carcinogenicity, genotoxicity, and developmental/reproductive toxicity studies were not conducted.

Source: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/product-information/kimmtrak-epar-product-information_en.pdf

### Therapeutic Goods Administration

The Australian AusPAR states that omission of toxicity studies in animal species was justified by the human specificity of tebentafusp and the absence of equivalent targets in other species. The same assessment describes the human gp100/HLA-A*02:01 and CD3 binding data, human tissue-panel evidence, T-cell activation/cytokine evidence and the limited relevance of nonclinical PK because appropriate animal models were absent. Genotoxicity and carcinogenicity studies were not conducted in line with ICH S6(R1), and reproductive risk was addressed using published literature/target biology.

AusPAR: https://www.tga.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-11/auspar-kimmtrak-221025.pdf
TGA decision page: https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/auspmd/kimmtrak

### Analytical significance

Kimmtrak should now be treated as a **cross-jurisdiction named precedent**, not an FDA-only anecdote. The same core scientific constraint—human specificity/no relevant species—and a human-centered alternative evidence strategy were recognized by three major regulators. This strengthens the paper's convergence argument while remaining different from a formal mutual-recognition process or a single shared dossier decision.

## Carvykti: EMA acceptance without conventional formal animal toxicology

EMA's CHMP assessment report for Carvykti (ciltacabtagene autoleucel) provides a second high-value named product precedent in a different modality.

The assessment states that the nonclinical safety evaluation was limited because there was no relevant animal model. No GLP-compliant formal toxicology studies were performed. EMA considered the absence of repeat-dose toxicity studies acceptable because Carvykti is a patient-specific product unsuitable for administration to immunocompetent animals. Although the applicant had conducted one non-GLP cynomolgus-monkey safety study using related CAR-T cells, the monkey model was considered non-relevant because the construct did not bind cynomolgus BCMA. No other in-vivo nonclinical safety evaluation was performed. On-target/off-tumour and off-target risks were addressed using literature-based assessment, in-vitro studies and clinical monitoring/risk management.

Source: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/assessment-report/carvykti-epar-public-assessment-report_en.pdf

Analytical coding: **named animal-displacement precedent** because conventional formal GLP/repeat-dose toxicology was omitted in an approved product, with explicit regulator acceptance of the nonclinical strategy. This is not an animal-free development program because some in-vivo pharmacology and a non-GLP monkey study were conducted.

## PMDA: explicit NHP DART replacement pathway

On October 24, 2025, PMDA issued an Early Consideration titled *Use of the Weight of Evidence approach as an alternative to nonhuman primate developmental and reproductive toxicity testing for biopharmaceuticals*.

Source: https://www.pmda.go.jp/files/000278904.pdf
PMDA NAM hub: https://www.pmda.go.jp/english/review-services/0022.html

PMDA states that NHPs are often the only relevant species for highly target/species-specific biopharmaceuticals but that NHP DART studies have important scientific limitations. The document explicitly says that even when a biopharmaceutical is pharmacologically active only in NHPs, an alternative scheme can be used **in place of NHPs** with appropriate scientific justification. The WOE pathway can integrate mechanism and target biology, placental transfer, literature/databases, human data from similar drugs, existing genetically modified-animal information and other evidence. PMDA also gives circumstances in which available evidence may render DART testing unnecessary and describes alternative models when additional testing is needed.

Status discipline is essential: PMDA labels this **Early Consideration**, describing it as reference information/current thinking for emerging technology that may change. It is not binding final guidance.

Analytical coding: major international adoption-context signal, `early_consideration_nonbinding`, not a completed named-product adoption event.

## PMDA: rationalizing six-month monkey toxicity for monoclonal antibodies

On May 13, 2026, PMDA issued *Considerations for the General Toxicity Evaluation of Monoclonal Antibodies (Early Consideration)*.

Source: https://www.pmda.go.jp/files/000280938.pdf

PMDA notes that monkeys are often the only relevant species for conventional monoclonal antibodies, but retrospective comparisons of shorter and longer monkey studies suggest that three-month studies can often provide sufficient information. For deciding whether a six-month repeated-dose study is necessary, PMDA identifies WOE inputs including the three-month monkey study, mechanism and target biology, literature/databases, NAM data, clinical safety/PK and class experience. Where specified risk conditions do not apply—or where immunogenicity makes longer monkey studies difficult to interpret—the additional value of a six-month study may be limited and scientific merit should be considered case-by-case. PMDA also discusses NAM/target-safety approaches where no appropriate animal species exists.

Again, this is **Early Consideration**, not binding final guidance.

## Emerging international pattern

The international evidence now supports a more specific convergence claim than “regulators are interested in NAMs.” Across FDA, EMA, TGA and PMDA, a shared regulatory logic is becoming visible:

1. Ask whether an animal model is scientifically relevant for the human mechanism.
2. Avoid treating non-relevant species as automatically informative.
3. Use human tissue/cell data, NAMs, mechanism/target biology, existing evidence and clinical information to address defined uncertainties.
4. Shorten or omit longer studies when incremental information value is low.
5. Preserve consultation and case-specific justification where the pathway is not yet routinized.

The maturity differs sharply by record: Kimmtrak and Carvykti are named approved-product precedents; PMDA's 2025/2026 documents are nonbinding prospective regulatory positions. These categories must remain separate in quantitative analysis.

## Manuscript implications

This pass strengthens three eventual arguments:

- **Species relevance is becoming an explicit regulatory decision variable**, not merely an ethical argument for reduction.
- **Convergence can precede formal harmonization**: regulators can independently arrive at similar evidentiary logic even without one shared qualification decision.
- **Adoption maturity is layered**: named product decisions, final guidance, draft guidance and Early Consideration documents should be displayed as distinct evidentiary states rather than one adoption category.

Kimmtrak may eventually deserve a short cross-jurisdiction case box or figure because it demonstrates the same scientific problem and alternative evidence strategy across FDA, EMA and TGA. Carvykti provides a complementary advanced-therapy example.
