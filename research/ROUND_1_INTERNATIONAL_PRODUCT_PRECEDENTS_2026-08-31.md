# Research Round 1 — International product precedents and regulatory streamlining

Date: 2026-08-31

## Objective

Test whether the emerging U.S. adoption findings are jurisdiction-specific or whether the same evidentiary logic appears in public assessment records and regulator positions outside the United States. Preserve strict distinctions among named product decisions, current regulatory practice, final guidance, draft guidance, nonbinding regulator thinking, and prospective targets.

## Kimmtrak as a four-regulator cross-jurisdiction case

Kimmtrak (tebentafusp) is now supported by public records from FDA, EMA, TGA, and Health Canada.

### FDA

FDA/CDER/OND reviewers identify Kimmtrak as a NAM regulatory-decision example. Human tissue and cell-based assays assessed target/species specificity, human-tissue cross-reactivity, and organ-specific toxicity potential. FDA authors state that these data supported clinical-trial safety in the absence of pharmacologically relevant animal models.

FDA review: https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2022/761228Orig1s000MultidisciplineR.pdf
FDA-authored synthesis: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10915818251384270

### European Medicines Agency

EMA product information states that tebentafusp is human-specific and that there are no relevant animal species in which nonclinical toxicology could be tested. It records that carcinogenicity, genotoxicity, and developmental/reproductive toxicity studies were not conducted.

Source: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/product-information/kimmtrak-epar-product-information_en.pdf

### Therapeutic Goods Administration

The Australian AusPAR states that omission of toxicity studies in animal species was justified by the human specificity of tebentafusp and the absence of equivalent targets in other species. The assessment describes the human gp100/HLA-A*02:01 and CD3 binding data, human tissue-panel evidence, T-cell activation/cytokine evidence, and the limited relevance of nonclinical PK because appropriate animal models were absent. Genotoxicity and carcinogenicity studies were not conducted in line with ICH S6(R1), and reproductive risk was addressed using published literature/target biology.

AusPAR: https://www.tga.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-11/auspar-kimmtrak-221025.pdf
TGA decision page: https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/auspmd/kimmtrak

### Health Canada

Health Canada's Summary Basis of Decision independently states that tebentafusp is a human-specific protein and that there were no relevant animal species in which its nonclinical toxicology could be evaluated. No long-term carcinogenicity, genotoxicity, or reproductive/developmental toxicity studies were conducted. Health Canada explicitly notes that although foreign Project Orbis reviews were used as added references, the Canadian regulatory decision was made independently and was based on the Canadian review.

Source: https://dhpp.hpfb-dgpsa.ca/review-documents/resource/SBD00605

### Analytical significance

Kimmtrak should now be treated as a **four-regulator independent-convergence precedent**, not an FDA-only anecdote. The same core scientific constraint—human specificity/no relevant species—and corresponding alternative evidence strategy were recognized independently in the United States, European Union, Australia, and Canada.

This is stronger than parallel rhetoric but still different from formal harmonization, mutual recognition, or one shared multinational regulatory decision. The case therefore supports the manuscript claim that **convergence can occur at the level of evidentiary logic before formal harmonization**.

## Carvykti: EMA acceptance without conventional formal animal toxicology

EMA's CHMP assessment report for Carvykti (ciltacabtagene autoleucel) provides a high-value named product precedent in an advanced-therapy modality.

The assessment states that the nonclinical safety evaluation was limited because there was no relevant animal model. No GLP-compliant formal toxicology studies were performed. EMA considered the absence of repeat-dose toxicity studies acceptable because Carvykti is a patient-specific product unsuitable for administration to immunocompetent animals. Although one non-GLP cynomolgus-monkey safety study using related CAR-T cells existed, the monkey model was considered non-relevant because the construct did not bind cynomolgus BCMA. No other in-vivo nonclinical safety evaluation was performed. On-target/off-tumour and off-target risks were addressed using literature-based assessment, in-vitro studies, and clinical monitoring/risk management.

Source: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/assessment-report/carvykti-epar-public-assessment-report_en.pdf

Analytical coding: **named animal-displacement precedent** because conventional formal GLP/repeat-dose toxicology was omitted in an approved product with explicit regulator acceptance of the strategy. This was not an animal-free program because some in-vivo pharmacology and a non-GLP monkey study were conducted.

Health Canada's later Carvykti authorization independently documents human-cell in-vitro functional assays and in-vitro assessment of insertional-mutagenesis risk. It does not make the same explicit no-relevant-model/repeat-dose-omission statement and is therefore supporting NAM-adoption evidence rather than an independent confirmation of the EMA displacement decision.

Health Canada source: https://dhpp.hpfb-dgpsa.ca/review-documents/resource/SBD1684955300002/

## Sarclisa (isatuximab): strong named lead with partial primary-source confirmation

Sarclisa is a useful test of how carefully the project must distinguish **species-limited development**, **specific omitted studies**, and **human-tissue NAM reliance**.

Health Canada's original Summary Basis of Decision states that the nonclinical evaluation was limited by isatuximab's species specificity. It records that effects on embryofetal development were not investigated because appropriate animal models were absent, while also stating that the main toxicity study was GLP-compliant. This means Sarclisa is **not** an example of wholesale omission of animal toxicology.

Health Canada also records that the nonclinical review was conducted through an Australia-Canada-Singapore-Switzerland work-sharing initiative: TGA led the clinical and nonclinical review, Health Canada led quality/RMP review, and each jurisdiction made its own authorization decision.

Health Canada source: https://dhpp.hpfb-dgpsa.ca/review-documents/resource/SBD00509

EMA's current risk-management material is indexed with a stronger statement: target-organ identification could not be performed in animals because no relevant species or surrogate antibody existed and was therefore based on in-vitro studies using human tissues. That would make Sarclisa a particularly useful **named human-tissue regulatory-reliance** example. However, the relevant EMA source is a PDF and direct access was rate-limited during this pass, preventing the required page-image verification. **Do not promote the human-tissue claim into the verified named-adoption ledger until the relevant EMA page has been visually inspected.**

Provisional EMA source: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/rmp/sarclisa-epar-risk-management-plan_en.pdf

Current coding recommendation: retain as a high-priority candidate. Health Canada already supports a named study-omission/species-relevance claim for embryofetal development; the broader human-tissue NAM-reliance claim remains pending direct PDF verification.

## PMDA: explicit NHP DART replacement pathway

On October 24, 2025, PMDA issued an Early Consideration titled *Use of the Weight of Evidence approach as an alternative to nonhuman primate developmental and reproductive toxicity testing for biopharmaceuticals*.

Source: https://www.pmda.go.jp/files/000278904.pdf
PMDA NAM hub: https://www.pmda.go.jp/english/review-services/0022.html

PMDA states that NHPs are often the only relevant species for highly target/species-specific biopharmaceuticals but that NHP DART studies have important scientific limitations. The document explicitly says that even when a biopharmaceutical is pharmacologically active only in NHPs, an alternative scheme can be used **in place of NHPs** with appropriate scientific justification. The WOE pathway can integrate mechanism and target biology, placental transfer, literature/databases, human data from similar drugs, existing genetically modified-animal information, and other evidence.

Status discipline is essential: PMDA labels this **Early Consideration**, describing it as reference information/current thinking for emerging technology that may change. It is not binding final guidance.

## PMDA: rationalizing six-month monkey toxicity for monoclonal antibodies

On May 13, 2026, PMDA issued *Considerations for the General Toxicity Evaluation of Monoclonal Antibodies (Early Consideration)*.

Source: https://www.pmda.go.jp/files/000280938.pdf

PMDA notes that monkeys are often the only relevant species for conventional monoclonal antibodies, but retrospective comparisons of shorter and longer monkey studies suggest that three-month studies can often provide sufficient information. For deciding whether a six-month repeated-dose study is necessary, PMDA identifies WOE inputs including the three-month monkey study, mechanism and target biology, literature/databases, NAM data, clinical safety/PK, and class experience. Where specified risk conditions do not apply—or where immunogenicity makes longer monkey studies difficult to interpret—the additional value of a six-month study may be limited and scientific merit should be considered case-by-case.

Again, this is **Early Consideration**, not binding final guidance.

## United Kingdom: current practice versus future formalization

The UK government's November 2025 *Replacing animals in science* strategy contains unusually useful statements about what is already happening and what remains a future target.

Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/replacing-animals-in-science-strategy/replacing-animals-in-science-a-strategy-to-support-the-development-validation-and-uptake-of-alternative-methods

### Current biologicals practice

The strategy states that UK regulators already apply case-by-case judgment for biological therapies where no pharmacologically relevant animal model exists. It says that a small number of biological drugs with well-defined mechanisms have entered first-in-human development using a battery of in-vitro tests and a weight-of-evidence strategy rather than the usual animal package.

The strategy also states that UK, U.S., EU, and Australian regulators have approved clinical trials of biological therapies without standard preclinical animal-data packages in selected cases.

This is a **direct government statement of current regulatory practice**, but it does not identify the products. Therefore it belongs in the regulatory-context layer, not the named-product ledger.

The future target is distinct: by the end of 2026 the UK intends to define and apply guidance explicitly permitting first-in-human submissions for biological therapies with no pharmacologically relevant animal model based only on non-animal data. Do not treat this target as already completed.

### Botulinum toxin potency testing

The UK strategy states that MHRA **now accepts a non-animal alternative method** for testing specified common strengths of Type A botulinum-toxin drug substances (500U BAS and 300U BAS) and that since 2024 it no longer licenses LD50 testing for third countries. Traditional batch-potency LD50 testing can use groups of up to 100 mice per test.

This is a particularly strong mature adoption context because it is an implemented quality-control replacement rather than a research or draft-guidance proposal.

The future target is again separate: by the end of 2027 the UK aims to use only validated alternative methods for pharmacopoeial botulinum-toxin potency testing except rare safety incidents where no validated alternative can answer the question.

### Why the UK evidence matters

The UK material supports two manuscript-relevant points:

1. adoption can predate explicit formal guidance, operating through case-by-case scientific judgment; and
2. eventual formalization can be understood as **routinization of an already demonstrated exception pathway**, rather than the first moment an alternative becomes possible.

That is closely aligned with the emerging routinization concept from EPA.

## Emerging international pattern

The international evidence now supports a more specific convergence claim than “regulators are interested in NAMs.” Across FDA, EMA, TGA, Health Canada, PMDA, and UK practice, a shared regulatory logic is becoming visible:

1. Ask whether an animal model is scientifically relevant for the human mechanism.
2. Avoid treating non-relevant species as automatically informative.
3. Use human tissue/cell data, NAMs, mechanism/target biology, existing evidence, and clinical information to address defined uncertainties.
4. Shorten or omit studies when incremental information value is low.
5. Use case-specific consultation where the pathway is not yet routinized.
6. Formalize repeatedly accepted exceptions into guidance, standards, or ordinary review practice once sufficient precedent accumulates.

The maturity differs sharply by record: Kimmtrak and Carvykti are named approved-product precedents; UK botulinum potency replacement is current implemented practice; UK biologicals use is current but unnamed case-by-case practice; PMDA's 2025/2026 documents are nonbinding prospective positions; and the UK 2026/2027 milestones remain future targets.

## Manuscript implications

This pass strengthens four eventual arguments:

- **Species relevance is becoming an explicit regulatory decision variable**, not merely an ethical argument for reduction.
- **Convergence can precede formal harmonization**: regulators can independently arrive at similar evidentiary logic without one shared qualification decision.
- **Adoption maturity is layered**: named product decisions, routine regulator practice, final guidance, draft guidance, Early Consideration, and prospective targets should be separate evidentiary states.
- **Formalization may be a late-stage marker of routinization** rather than the beginning of adoption. The UK explicitly describes current case-by-case use followed by planned formal guidance, paralleling EPA's movement from specialist council review to ordinary branch-level decisions.

Kimmtrak now has enough cross-jurisdiction evidence to be a serious candidate for a short paper case box or figure after the contemporary corpus is frozen. Carvykti provides a complementary advanced-therapy example. Sarclisa may become a third useful example once the EMA human-tissue evidence is directly verified.
