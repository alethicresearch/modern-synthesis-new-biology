# Research Round 1 — Regulator-verified NAM adoption and displacement metrics

Date: 2026-08-31

## Objective

Move beyond policy announcements and sponsor claims by identifying regulator-authored evidence of named NAM use in actual product development, and quantified public measures of animal-study avoidance.

## FDA/CDER/OND regulator-authored synthesis

A 2026 article authored by FDA/CDER/OND Pharmacology/Toxicology reviewers provides an unusually valuable bridge between general policy and product-level regulatory practice. The authors state that NAMs have historically been incorporated into standard nonclinical assessment and identify named examples drawn from public Drugs@FDA approval packages.

Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10915818251384270

### Kimmtrak (tebentafusp-tebn)

FDA authors describe human tissue and cell-based in vitro assays that assessed target/species specificity, cross-reactivity across 35 human tissues, and organ-specific toxicity potential across 13 tissues. They state that these assays supported the safety of clinical trials in the absence of pharmacologically relevant animal models and that development subsequently led to approval.

Analytical coding: this is a confirmed named human-based regulatory-adoption case and should count in the stricter animal-displacement layer, but the wording should remain precise. It demonstrates human-based evidence supporting development where a relevant animal model was unavailable; it is not presented as a one-for-one waiver of a named mandatory animal study.

FDA approval package cited by the FDA-authored paper: https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2022/761228Orig1s000MultidisciplineR.pdf

### Veopoz (pozelimab-bbfg)

FDA authors report that no pharmacologically relevant species existed for the key complement pharmacology. Cell-based assays and a validated ex vivo CH50 hemolysis assay provided mechanistic and pharmacodynamic evidence; together with correlating clinical evidence, the NAM data supported approval.

Analytical coding: confirmed named NAM adoption, but not pure animal-study displacement because FDA's totality-of-evidence discussion includes animal, healthy-volunteer, and patient data.

### Kalydeco (ivacaftor)

FDA accepted in vitro CFTR chloride-transport assays to identify treatment-responsive variants and expand the indicated genetic population without requiring separate additional clinical trials for each variant.

Analytical coding: major NAM adoption precedent but not animal displacement. It is evidence substitution at the clinical-evidence layer and should be counted separately.

FDA orphan/approval record: https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/opdlisting/oopd/detailedIndex.cfm?cfgridkey=228306

### Galafold (migalastat)

FDA accepted in vitro alpha-galactosidase A activity thresholds to identify amenable GLA variants, supporting expansion without pre-approval clinical trials for every variant; residual uncertainty was handled through a post-approval clinical verification requirement.

Analytical coding: major NAM adoption precedent but not animal displacement. It is particularly useful for the manuscript's argument that regulatory reliance can be staged and conditional.

FDA orphan/approval record: https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/opdlisting/oopd/detailedIndex.cfm?cfgridkey=180003

## FDA quantitative displacement: carcinogenicity

The FDA/CDER/OND authors state that under the ICH S1B(R1) weight-of-evidence pathway, when a two-year rat carcinogenicity study is not conducted, more than 400 animals are not used, depending on study design.

This is useful as a per-decision displacement estimate. It must not be converted into a national total without a denominator for the number of studies actually omitted.

## EPA: realized animal-reduction metrics

EPA Office of Pesticide Programs publishes unusually strong realized metrics rather than only prospective estimates.

Source: https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-science-and-assessing-pesticide-risks/strategic-vision-adopting-new-approach-0

### Branch-level decisions, FY2023

EPA reports that registering divisions began capturing decisions where acute toxicity testing was not necessary. The category includes data citations, bridging requests, additional study waivers, and weight-of-evidence approaches.

FY2023:
- 1,122 studies saved
- 9,415–18,026 animals reduced
- $12,004,500 estimated study-cost savings
- $1,102,702 EPA review-cost savings

This is currently one of the strongest public quantitative adoption records in the corpus. It is broader than human-based NAM substitution and should therefore be labelled as broader evidence-based testing reduction.

### Acute dermal waiver program, FY2018–FY2023

EPA's annual acute-dermal waiver table reports 262 granted waivers cumulatively over FY2018–FY2023. Summing the agency's annual animal-reduction ranges yields 2,620–7,860 animals reduced over those six years.

This converts the 2016 prospective policy estimate into observed agency-tracked implementation.

### CATSAC and organizational learning

EPA notes that beginning in FY2022, most acute-toxicity determinations could be made at the branch level without CATSAC consultation, with the council increasingly reserved for complex arguments and rebuttals. This is analytically important: successful adoption may alter organizational workflow, moving once-specialized waiver decisions into routine review practice.

That suggests an additional adoption indicator beyond animal counts: **institutional routinization** — whether an alternative or waiver pathway migrates from specialist review to ordinary branch-level decision-making.

## Emerging empirical framework

The corpus should now distinguish at least four outcomes:

1. **Animal-study displacement** — an animal study is omitted, replaced, shortened, or avoided.
2. **NAM regulatory reliance** — a NAM materially supports a named regulatory decision, even if it does not replace an animal study.
3. **Human-evidence/clinical-study substitution** — NAM evidence substitutes for otherwise impractical clinical evidence, as in genotype-specific indication expansion.
4. **Institutional routinization** — a once-exceptional alternative pathway becomes ordinary enough to be handled in standard review workflows.

These should not be collapsed into a single adoption count.

## Manuscript implications

The paper's Commitment → Qualification → Adoption framework can now be made more empirical. Adoption appears multidimensional rather than binary. A useful later figure could show four adoption outputs downstream of qualification: animal displacement, named regulatory reliance, evidence substitution, and routinization.

The strongest immediate evidence for the paper is no longer simply that agencies endorse NAMs. It is that:

- FDA reviewers can identify named approved products in which NAM evidence had specific regulatory impacts;
- EPA reports realized study and animal reductions with denominators; and
- routinization can be observed when waiver decisions move from specialist councils into ordinary branch-level review.

This materially strengthens the argument that public reporting of contexts, outcomes, and denominators is feasible and that other agencies could publish comparable evidence.
